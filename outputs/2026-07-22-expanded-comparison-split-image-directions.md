# Expanded split-image directions: transitional vs. transformational thinking

## Shared art direction

Each comparison should be one unified 50/50 split image. The two halves should share the same camera height, perspective, realism, historical period, and general color treatment so the difference reads as a difference in thinking rather than an unrelated before-and-after montage.

On the **transitional left half**, make the legacy system dominant and recognizable. Preserve its physical layout, participants, workflow, institutions, and underlying operating model. Introduce only the specific improved tool or channel described in the comparison. The viewer should be able to point to most of the scene and say, “that stayed the same,” then identify one localized change.

On the **transformational right half**, show the technology reorganizing several connected parts of the system at once. Use visible flows of people, materials, information, energy, or transactions to make coordination legible. The right side should communicate a new operating model, not merely a larger, cleaner, or more futuristic version of the left.

Keep the main visual evidence in the lower two-thirds and toward the outer edges. Preserve calm, low-detail areas behind the future text overlay. Do not place words, signs, labels, logos, branded products, readable screens, numbers, or watermarks in the image.

## Steam power

### Manufacturing

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the old timber mill building, narrow floor plan, existing machine positions, manual handoffs, long overhead drive shaft, leather belts, and workers performing the same tasks in the same sequence. Retain the mill's cramped, workshop-like character.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Replace only the exterior water wheel and water-driven axle with a compact steam engine connected to the existing central shaft. Make the new engine visibly feed the old belt system; do not rearrange the machinery or workforce. A dry or unreliable millrace beside the building can subtly explain why steam is more dependable.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show a purpose-built multi-bay factory organized around a large central steam-power hall. Arrange specialized production zones in a clear sequence: raw material arriving at one end, preparation and machine work in the middle, assembly and inspection farther along, and finished goods leaving from the other end. Include coordinated teams, foremen with visual sightlines across the floor, material carts moving between zones, synchronized machines, storage areas matched to production stages, tall windows, and repeated structural bays that imply scale. The steam engine should no longer be the sole hero; the organized factory system it enables should dominate.

### Agriculture

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve small irregular fields divided by hedges or fences, traditional crop placement, horse-drawn carts, scattered farm buildings, seasonal labor crews, and the existing sequence of fieldwork. Keep land ownership and farm organization visually small-scale and fragmented.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Add one portable steam engine powering a stationary thresher or irrigation pump. Workers should still bring crops to the machine by hand or horse cart and continue the remaining tasks manually. The steam equipment improves one bottleneck while every surrounding field pattern and work relationship remains unchanged.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Use a broad elevated view of large contiguous fields laid out for machine access, with straight boundaries and planned routes between cultivation, harvesting, processing, storage, and transport. Show coordinated steam-powered machinery working across several fields, mobile crews assigned to different stages, a centralized threshing or processing building, grain silos, maintenance sheds, organized loading yards, and a rail spur or heavy transport connection carrying output to market. Repeated machine paths and simultaneous activity should make the farm read as one mechanized production system.

### Rail transport

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve an isolated point-to-point route, a small local station, nonstandard track infrastructure, manual loading, an independent timetable, and limited connections beyond the line. Keep the surrounding towns, freight transfer methods, and route organization unchanged.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Make the locomotive newer, faster, and more powerful, pulling passengers and freight at visible speed along the same single route. Use motion, steam, and blurred countryside to emphasize vehicle performance, while the station and operating system remain modest and disconnected.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show a high, expansive view of a standardized rail network centered on a major junction. Include parallel tracks, switching points, coordinated arrivals and departures, passenger platforms, a freight classification yard, warehouses, cranes transferring goods, maintenance facilities, and lines branching toward a city, factory district, port, and distant towns. Several trains should serve different functions at once. Repeated station architecture, shared track gauge, synchronized platform activity, and seamless freight transfers should make the network—not any single locomotive—the central subject.

## Electricity

### Manufacturing

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the traditional factory's rigid rows of machines, central line shaft, overhead pulleys, belts, fixed workstations, batch workflow, dark interior, and original sequence of labor. Workers and materials should navigate the same awkward layout.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Replace the steam engine that formerly drove the shaft with one large electric motor attached to that same shaft. The motor should be visibly cleaner and more compact, but the belts still descend from the ceiling to every machine. Nothing about product flow or workstation arrangement changes.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show an open, bright production floor in which each machine has its own small electric motor and can therefore be positioned according to the sequence of work. Arrange stations from component preparation through assembly, finishing, inspection, and packing. Add a moving conveyor or clearly continuous material path, standardized work areas, parts arriving exactly where needed, workers specializing by stage, overhead lighting independent of windows, and clear aisles for replenishment. The building footprint, machine placement, and workforce coordination should all follow production flow.

### The home

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the old household layout, manual laundry area, pantry or icebox, coal or wood stove, washbasin, clothesline, daily schedule, and one-chore-at-a-time domestic routine. Keep most tools visibly manual.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Electrify only one familiar implement, such as an iron plugged into a wall outlet. The person still heats water manually, washes by hand, hangs clothing to dry, stores food traditionally, and moves through the same sequence of chores. The single appliance reduces effort without reorganizing domestic life.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Use a carefully composed cutaway view through several connected rooms of an electric home. Show a refrigerator changing food storage and shopping frequency, an electric range changing meal preparation, a washing machine handling laundry while another task occurs, bright electric lighting extending usable hours, a vacuum or other cleaning appliance, hot water service, and outlets distributed throughout the home. Show family members performing overlapping activities rather than waiting on sequential manual chores. Electrical service should appear as an invisible shared foundation connecting the rooms, with a cleaner kitchen, dedicated utility area, and routines reorganized around simultaneous appliance use.

### Communication

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve staffed telegraph offices at both ends, trained operators, coded transmission, paper message slips, messengers carrying the final note, and one-way or delayed written communication. The public should remain separated from the electrical equipment.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Upgrade only the telegraph key, receiver, or line so an operator can transmit the same coded message faster. Show the improved apparatus as one refined component within the unchanged office-to-office workflow.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show several ordinary people speaking directly and simultaneously from a home, office, shop, hotel, and public telephone. Connect them visually through branching local lines leading to a busy telephone exchange where operators route many live conversations, then onward through shared trunk lines linking neighborhoods or cities. Include callers reacting in real time, multiple concurrent connections, wall and desk telephones embedded in daily settings, and an exchange board alive with activity. The visual story is direct conversation made possible by shared infrastructure.

### Power distribution

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve a small neighborhood-scale lighting service, a local generator close to customers, short distribution wires, a limited service radius, and buildings beyond the district still using gas lamps or remaining dark. Electricity serves one local purpose rather than the wider economy.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Replace nearby gas streetlights or interior lamps with brighter electric bulbs supplied by the local generator. Concentrate the improvement within a few adjacent streets and allow the light to visibly fade at the edge of the service area.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Create a sweeping dusk landscape that can be read from generation to use. In the distance, show a large generating station; then high-voltage lines crossing long terrain, transformers stepping voltage between levels, substations feeding branching regional lines, and local distribution reaching factories, transit, businesses, farms, streets, and homes. Different kinds of customers should glow across several towns and landscapes. Use elegant branching lines and repeated points of illumination to make generation, transmission, transformation, and distribution feel like one regional utility.

### Vertical transportation

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve a low, stair-centered building with floors arranged around a grand staircase, upper levels that are harder to reach, people climbing on foot, and the same basic horizontal architectural logic.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Replace gas lamps along the stairs with bright electric fixtures. Add clearer visibility and safer landings, but keep every person using the same stair route and keep the building's height and floor organization unchanged.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show a tall early skyscraper in partial sectional perspective, organized around a bank of electric elevator shafts. Multiple cars should carry groups rapidly between a busy lobby, middle offices, and high floors. Include compact stairwells relegated to a secondary role, dense rentable floors surrounding the elevator core, people entering and leaving at different levels, vertical service shafts, and a city skyline growing upward around the new movement system. The building's height, floor value, occupancy, and circulation should visibly depend on routine elevator travel.

### Motion pictures

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the vaudeville theater, live stage, performers, orchestra, curtains, fixed performance schedule, local audience, and program built around a succession of live acts.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Add a short projected film as one novelty between live performances. Keep the screen modest, the projection brief, and the surrounding stage machinery and performers visually dominant. The theater still sells a live program.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Compose a continuous visual chain from production to mass exhibition: a purpose-built studio set with camera crew and actors, film processing and editing, identical reels being duplicated and packed, distribution crates moving by rail or truck, and multiple cinemas presenting the same recorded story to large audiences in different places. Show scheduled screenings, projection booths, purpose-built screens, poster spaces without readable content, and audiences focused entirely on the film rather than waiting for a live act. Repetition of the same abstract screen imagery across cinemas should communicate scalable distribution.

## Computing

### Retail operations

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve independent stores, store-specific stock decisions, manual shelf counts, paper purchase orders, separate warehouses, periodic replenishment, and the existing relationship with suppliers. Keep the computer isolated from the sales floor and inventory movement.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Place a large early computer in the back office processing payroll and accounting records faster. Staff should still count stock manually, telephone orders to the warehouse, and make replenishment decisions store by store. Only administrative paperwork becomes digital.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show several stores, a regional distribution center, supplier facilities, and delivery vehicles functioning as one connected operation. At a checkout, a product leaves the shelf; represent that event with a subtle non-textual pulse traveling to shared inventory, triggering a warehouse pick, consolidated replenishment, and a supplier production response. Include organized storage locations, coordinated delivery routes, cross-store stock visibility, centralized purchasing, and products flowing toward locations where demand is rising. Avoid dashboards; let synchronized physical actions make shared information visible.

### Enterprise operations

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve departmental silos, separate approval chains, duplicate records, paper handoffs, local filing systems, and independent ways of working in finance, purchasing, production, inventory, human resources, and sales.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Give each department its own computer for faster record entry and retrieval. Show paper or messengers still moving between rooms, duplicated files being re-entered, and managers continuing to reconcile conflicting information manually.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Arrange the enterprise as a connected sequence around a shared information backbone. A sales order should initiate linked activity: finance checks terms, procurement secures inputs, manufacturing schedules work, inventory allocates materials, people operations confirms staffing, shipping prepares delivery, and finance records the outcome. Use one repeated visual token or pulse passing through each function, while materials move in parallel on the factory floor. Include cross-functional teams, shared identifiers represented without text, fewer duplicate handoffs, and managers seeing the same current state. The whole company should respond coherently to one event.

### Payments

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve a branch-centered bank, institution-specific records, teller counters, internal settlement routines, paper documentation, closed customer relationships, and limited connectivity to other banks or merchants.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Add a central computer that posts transactions, balances accounts, and accelerates the bank's own back-office settlement. Customers still visit branches, merchants still handle payments through separate arrangements, and institutions remain operational islands.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show one customer purchase initiating a coordinated path across a merchant terminal, communications network, acquiring institution, authorization center, issuing bank, and final settlement between institutions. Surround that path with many other simultaneous purchases connecting shops, restaurants, travel, and remote locations across regions. Include shoppers, merchants, several banks, shared processing infrastructure, fraud or authorization checkpoints represented as visual gates, and clearing flows consolidating many transactions. Use clean pulses and network arcs without card brands, currency figures, or interface copy.

## The Internet

### Entertainment

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the DVD-rental model: physical discs, finite local inventory, warehouse shelves, individual rentals, packaging, postal or courier delivery, returns, and waiting between selection and viewing.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Add a basic online ordering step at the beginning. Show a customer choosing from a computer, but make the selected disc continue through the same shelf-picking, envelope, delivery vehicle, home player, and return process.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show a shared digital video library delivering a selected story instantly to a television, laptop, tablet, and phone across different settings. Include a household beginning on one device and continuing on another, viewers choosing on demand at different times, a content delivery network suggested through distributed server locations, and many homes receiving streams simultaneously. Use consistent abstract imagery on blank, non-readable screens to show continuity. Remove discs, delivery vehicles, return deadlines, and local-stock constraints entirely.

### Retail

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the physical store as the center of inventory, merchandising, pricing, staffing, and fulfillment. Keep the same product assortment, local stockroom, store-led operations, and customer proposition.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Add a small online storefront that sends orders to the existing store. Show staff printing or receiving an order, walking the same aisles, picking from local shelves, packing at a back counter, and shipping to the customer. The website is simply another entrance to the store.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show an online-first retail system spanning digital discovery, a vast centrally accessible assortment, personalized product pathways represented without text, secure payment, supplier feeds, automated fulfillment, inventory distributed across several facilities, sorting hubs, route planning, last-mile delivery, and a parcel arriving at the customer. Include items unavailable in any one physical store, robots or workers picking from tall organized storage, orders from many customers being consolidated and separated, and inventory moving closer to predicted demand. The visual center should be the coordinated fulfillment network responding to digital discovery.

### Knowledge

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve a closed group of credentialed editors, centralized editorial control, fixed article ownership, periodic editions, one-way publication, and the encyclopedia's book-like article structure.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Put the same expert-written pages on a computer or website, making access faster and search easier. Keep readers passive and keep all revisions flowing through the same small editorial office.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show diverse volunteers across countries and time zones contributing to one evolving network of knowledge. Several people should add new material, connect related topics, review changes, correct mistakes, discuss disputed points, and revise older entries while readers follow links into adjacent subjects. Represent articles as luminous, interlinked page forms with visible revision layers and branching connections, but no legible words. Include many small contributions converging into stable shared pages, with review and correction happening continuously rather than only at publication time.

### Urban mobility

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve the licensed taxi fleet, central depot, dispatcher, fixed service territory, taxi ranks, radio communication, shift structure, and company-owned supply of cars.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Add an online reservation form that sends a booking to the same dispatch office. Show the dispatcher assigning one fleet vehicle by radio, with the rider still waiting for that traditional chain to complete.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Use an elevated city view in which riders requesting trips and independent nearby drivers become visible as paired points across several neighborhoods. Show the system continuously matching proximity and direction, updating routes around congestion, moving drivers toward areas of demand, confirming pickup, completing payment, and releasing the driver for the next match. Include many simultaneous trips, changing concentrations at a station or event, different vehicle locations, live route adjustments, and supply spreading through the city. The scene should feel like a responsive marketplace rather than a digitized dispatch office.

### Music

**Transitional half — what stays as is:** Preserve individual track ownership, per-item purchase, album and song units, a personal local library, downloaded files stored on one device, manual transfer, and discovery organized around buying a specific recording.

**Transitional half — what changes:** Replace the physical record shop or mail order with an online store and digital download. Show one purchased track moving into the customer's local collection, while every other recording remains separately selected, bought, downloaded, stored, and managed.

**Transformational half — specific visual direction:** Show a vast shared music catalog available continuously through a subscription-like access relationship, flowing to a listener's phone, headphones, home speaker, car, and laptop without manual file transfer. Depict the listener moving between environments while playback continues, then branching naturally from one artist or mood into related discoveries through subtle visual pathways. Include many listeners accessing the same catalog simultaneously, personal listening sequences forming dynamically, saved access without visible ownership objects, and new recommendations responding to behavior. Avoid album art and interfaces; contrast isolated file objects on the left with an abundant, continuous stream on the right.
