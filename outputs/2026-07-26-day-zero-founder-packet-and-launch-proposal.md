# Day-zero founder packet and launch proposal

## Outcome

Created the documentation set Bridge would have benefited from at inception, while retaining the repository’s Living Software pivot, canonical vocabulary, and existing detailed specifications.

The new packet covers:

- founder thesis and non-goals;
- product requirements and first-value journey;
- Avatar and trust-first Onboarding;
- Engine, Modules, and platform evolution;
- Commons publication, installation, supply chain, and business model;
- governance, data residency, privacy, security, and prompt injection;
- technical architecture and operations;
- customer profile, pricing hypothesis, sales motion, and positioning;
- pilot charter, support, incident, backup, release, and offboarding;
- metrics, Agent evaluation, cost, and unit economics;
- legal, IP, compliance, and material risks;
- 90-day roadmap, resourcing, launch gates, and founder decisions.

## Commit archive

Created a business-language record for every one of the 512 unique commits visible across local refs:

- 463 commits belong to the checked-out product lineage;
- 49 are preserved on other local branches or archive refs;
- monthly files retain date, hash, author, original title, business classification, affected areas, lineage status, and a plain-language summary;
- a local generator makes the archive reproducible without network access.

## Go-live recommendation

Launch as a controlled, paid, 90-day founder-led design-partner pilot:

- one Organization and up to five users;
- desktop-first with bounded web access;
- Relationship + Task Manager + Bridge-curated Commons;
- one measurable customer workflow;
- DealPilot only when authorized Source credentials and Google OAuth are available and certified;
- explicit Human approval for external Actions;
- no public self-serve or unknown Commons publishers.

Pricing hypothesis: $5,000 onboarding plus $2,500 per month, with the onboarding fee creditable toward an annual conversion. This remains a founder decision, not an established market claim.

## Founder memo first-principles revision

The founder memo was subsequently rebuilt for a reader with no prior Bridge knowledge. It now:

- starts with ordinary work rather than platform vocabulary;
- defines the problem before the solution;
- explains Egg, Avatar, Modules, Engine, and Commons by the job each performs;
- distinguishes Commons from Bridge Cloud and a Registry Entry from the library itself;
- walks through ETA as the first client and DealPilot as the first domain expression;
- explains how a private improvement becomes a generalized Commons capability without moving customer data;
- closes with differentiation, operating principles, non-goals, and the broader ambition.
