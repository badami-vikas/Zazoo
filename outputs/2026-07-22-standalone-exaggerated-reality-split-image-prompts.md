# Standalone exaggerated-reality prompts for split images

Each prompt is self-contained. The intended style is hyperreal editorial illustration: recognizable people, objects, architecture, and historical details arranged with deliberately exaggerated scale, repetition, perspective, and visual metaphor. The result should feel like reality intensified for immediate understanding—not fantasy, parody, cartoon, or a literal infographic. Overlay copy should be added separately.

## Steam power

### 1. Manufacturing

Create a wide 16:9 hyperreal cinematic editorial illustration with a seamless 50/50 vertical split, exaggerating reality to compare two nineteenth-century uses of steam power. On the left, show an old timber mill squeezed into an almost impossibly narrow, tangled interior: dozens of machines remain locked in their original positions beneath an oversized web of shafts and sagging leather belts, workers repeat the same manual handoffs, and material doubles back through the cramped room. Change only one thing—a small steam engine has replaced the water wheel and is awkwardly bolted onto the old central shaft; it works powerfully, but the entire legacy mill still bends around the old workflow. On the right, explode the scale into a monumental purpose-built factory whose repeating bays recede toward the horizon. A central steam hall anchors an elegant sequence of raw-material intake, specialized machining, assembly, inspection, packing, and outbound loading. Coordinated teams, synchronized machines, material carts, and stage-specific storage create one sweeping forward flow, visually amplified like a river of production. Make the left feel like one new heart trapped inside an old body and the right feel like a new organism designed around that heart. Keep the historical machinery plausible while using dramatic scale and compressed perspective. Reserve calm, smoky upper space on both halves for copy. No text, labels, signs, logos, numbers, watermarks, cartoon styling, science-fiction elements, or visible dividing line.
