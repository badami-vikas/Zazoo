# Split-image directions for transitional vs. transformational thinking

## Shared visual system

- Use one seamless, balanced 50/50 composition with the same camera height, realism, color grade, and lighting on both sides.
- The left half should feel constrained, isolated, and incremental; the right half should feel coordinated, expansive, and systemic.
- Keep important subjects in the lower third and outer edges. Avoid visible words, signs, logos, watermarks, interface text, or split-screen labels.
- Let the dividing line communicate a change in organization, not merely a before-and-after change in technology.

Left: An old timber mill keeps its traditional layout of long shafts, belts, and closely packed machinery, with a compact steam engine simply replacing the water wheel at one edge. Right: A vast purpose-built factory radiates outward from a central steam power hall, with specialized work zones, coordinated workers, material carts, and production moving through a deliberate sequence. The left feels like one machine was swapped; the right reveals an entirely new organization of work.

### Agriculture

Left: A small traditional farm field where a portable steam engine powers a single thresher beside workers, horses, fences, and irregular plots that otherwise remain unchanged. Right: A broad aerial-oblique view of contiguous fields organized around mechanized harvesting, centralized processing, storage, and rail loading, with crews and machines operating as one coordinated agricultural system. Preserve open sky over both halves for copy.

### Rail transport

Left: A steam locomotive rushes along a single isolated track through open countryside, with the visual emphasis on the engine's speed. Right: A high, wide view of a major rail junction where standardized tracks connect stations, freight yards, ports, factories, and a distant city, with several trains moving in coordinated directions. The locomotive is the hero on the left; the connected network is the hero on the right.

## Electricity

### Manufacturing

Left: A dim traditional factory retains one ceiling-mounted motor driving the old maze of overhead shafts and leather belts, leaving machines locked into rigid rows. Right: A bright, open production floor gives each workstation its own compact motor, allowing machines to follow a clean sequential flow with products moving continuously between specialized stations. Use the ceiling area as quiet overlay space.

### The home

Left: A single domestic scene centered on one electrified iron in an otherwise old-fashioned kitchen or laundry room, with manual tools and routines still surrounding it. Right: A cutaway-like view through a modern home where refrigeration, washing, lighting, cooking, and cleaning appliances work together across connected rooms, visibly reshaping the household's daily rhythm. Keep walls and upper-room surfaces visually restrained for text.

### Communication

Left: A staffed telegraph office where an operator sends a coded message between two fixed desks, with paper slips and wires reinforcing a mediated, point-to-point exchange. Right: A wide view of many ordinary people speaking directly by telephone from homes, offices, and shops, all subtly connected through a central exchange and branching lines. The right side should read as a living web of simultaneous conversation, not merely a newer device.

### Power distribution

Left: A small local generator lights a compact evening street or cluster of nearby buildings, with illumination fading quickly beyond the district. Right: A sweeping dusk landscape connects a distant power station to transmission towers, substations, towns, factories, and homes glowing across an entire region. Show scale through depth and branching distribution while leaving uncluttered twilight sky for the overlay.

### Vertical transportation

Left: People climb a grand multi-story staircase made safer by electric lamps, but the building remains low and horizontally organized. Right: A tall early skyscraper is shown in sectional perspective, with multiple electric elevators carrying people rapidly between many floors and the architecture rising around the elevator core. The contrast is an improved climb versus a building made possible by vertical movement.

### Motion pictures

Left: A vaudeville stage with live performers, curtains, and musicians, while a small film projection appears as a novelty between acts. Right: A coordinated cinematic ecosystem stretches from a film studio and reels in distribution crates to a grand cinema screening the same recorded story for a large audience, suggesting repeatability and mass reach. Keep the projection screen and dark upper auditorium free of readable content.

## Computing

### Retail operations

Left: The back office of a single department store uses a large early computer for payroll and accounting, while the sales floor and stockroom remain visually separate and manually run. Right: Multiple stores, warehouses, suppliers, and delivery hubs form one connected operational landscape, with inventory moving in response to shared information across the whole chain. Use abstract light paths or coordinated movement rather than readable interface graphics.

### Enterprise operations

Left: Several isolated departments occupy separate rooms, each converting paper ledgers into its own computer records without changing how work passes between them. Right: Finance, procurement, manufacturing, inventory, people operations, and sales surround a shared central information backbone, with materials and decisions flowing smoothly across departmental boundaries. The left is a collection of digital islands; the right is one coordinated organism.

### Payments

Left: Inside one bank, a mainframe accelerates transaction processing while customers, tellers, and paper records remain centered on that single institution. Right: A globe-spanning web connects banks, merchants, shoppers, payment terminals, and settlement centers, with one purchase visibly traveling through authorization and settlement across the network. Keep the connections elegant and sparse, without numbers, card brands, or interface labels.

## The Internet

### Entertainment

Left: A person orders a DVD from a basic home computer while shelves, discs, packaging, and delivery vehicles remain the physical center of the experience. Right: A family moves fluidly between television, laptop, tablet, and phone while the same cinematic world arrives instantly across devices from a shared streaming service. Emphasize immediate access and continuity, using blank or abstract screens so the overlay remains dominant.

### Retail

Left: A conventional store has a small online-order desk attached to it, while shelves, local stock, and the physical storefront remain the core operation. Right: A broad integrated commerce system connects digital discovery at home with a vast fulfillment center, automated sorting, payments, delivery routes, and a parcel reaching the customer. The contrast should be a store with a website versus retail rebuilt around digital demand.

### Knowledge

Left: A traditional encyclopedia volume appears reproduced on a computer screen, with a small group of editors still controlling a one-way publishing process. Right: Diverse contributors around the world add, connect, review, and refine a luminous web of interlinked knowledge in real time. Use visual nodes, pages, and connecting threads without legible writing; the network and collective activity should carry the idea.

### Urban mobility

Left: A taxi company office receives an online reservation and relays it to one fixed fleet through a traditional dispatcher. Right: A living city map becomes a real urban scene where riders and independent drivers converge dynamically, routes update, rides are matched nearby, and movement adapts continuously across neighborhoods. Keep the upper cityscape or map field calm enough for overlay copy.

### Music

Left: A customer at a desktop computer purchases and downloads one track, surrounded by the familiar visual logic of individually owned albums and files. Right: Music flows continuously from an expansive shared catalog to a listener across phone, home speaker, car, and headphones, with subtle recommendation paths opening into new artists and moods. Avoid album art, interface text, or logos; convey ownership versus access through the physical isolation of files on the left and seamless continuity on the right.
