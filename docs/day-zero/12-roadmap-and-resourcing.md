# Roadmap and resourcing

## Roadmap principle

Sequence by user proof and irreversibility:

1. Trust and data boundaries.
2. One complete useful workflow.
3. Repeatability and support.
4. Shared platform leverage.
5. Distribution and ecosystem.
6. Broader autonomy and surfaces.

## Current evidence

The repository already proves more than a normal pre-pilot product:

- coherent actionable shell: complete;
- trust-first Onboarding: complete;
- movable macOS Avatar: complete;
- signed Commons install and end-to-end demo: complete;
- Relationship consolidation, Agent/Skill orchestration, View grammar, Task Manager, runtime taint, and database correctness: complete;
- public-safe hosted API/web and live Supabase pilot boundary: complete;
- DealPilot core exists but remains blocked on authorized real Source credential and Google OAuth evidence;
- cross-platform signing/mobile release remains blocked;
- browser companion is deferred;
- public marketplace and broad self-serve are not ready.

This supports a private design-partner pilot, not general availability.

## Recommended 90-day roadmap

### Weeks 0–2: launch control

- Decide first workflow, pilot customer, pricing, and success metric.
- Name owners for product, security, operations, customer success, and desktop.
- Close DealPilot credential/OAuth gate if DealPilot is the wedge; otherwise freeze it outside pilot scope.
- Produce signed desktop build and install/update instructions.
- Create customer data map, pilot contract pack, subprocessor list, and support channel.
- Rehearse backup, restore, incident suspension, and offboarding.
- Create a repeatable demo and fresh-Organization provisioning checklist.

### Weeks 3–4: internal production rehearsal

- Run the selected workflow daily on real internal work.
- Measure time to value, corrections, cost, support, and failures.
- Fix P0/P1 launch defects only.
- Verify no customer-private data reaches public-safe hosted surfaces or Commons.
- Exercise model outage and provider fallback.
- Hold a go/no-go review.

### Weeks 5–8: first design partner

- Onboard one Organization and no more than five users.
- Operate weekly outcome reviews.
- Keep all new integrations and capability changes approval-gated.
- Measure baseline and business result.
- Avoid unrelated Module expansion.
- Capture product gaps separately from customer-specific requests.

### Weeks 9–12: repeatability

- Onboard a second and third design partner only if support and security are stable.
- Reuse the same core workflow and capability package.
- Standardize provisioning, training, support, and reporting.
- Decide whether to convert, narrow the segment, or revise the wedge.

## Six-to-twelve-month sequence

1. Paid pilot repeatability.
2. Multi-user administration and tenant certification.
3. Desktop signing/updating and mobile approval client.
4. Model economics and Memory-quality improvement.
5. Second distinct Module proving platform leverage.
6. Invited Commons publishers and private catalogs.
7. Broader interaction modes.
8. Enterprise controls only after demand.

## Minimum accountable team

For a responsible pilot:

- Founder/product lead: segment, outcome, pricing, decisions.
- Platform engineer: Engine, API, deployment, reliability.
- Product/client engineer: desktop/web experience and accessibility.
- AI/evaluation engineer: model routing, prompts, evals, cost.
- Security/data owner: authority, privacy, incident, vendor review.
- Customer-success/operations owner: onboarding, support, measurement.

Some roles may be combined initially, but ownership cannot be absent. A sustainable three-customer pilot likely needs four to six full-time equivalent contributors plus fractional legal/security review.

## Capacity allocation

- 45% pilot reliability and workflow completion.
- 20% customer onboarding and support.
- 15% security, privacy, and release operations.
- 10% evaluation and cost.
- 10% reusable platform improvements.

Do not allocate meaningful capacity to a public marketplace, broad mobile parity, or speculative Modules during the first paid pilots.

## Decision gates

### Enter first external pilot

- one workflow, buyer, baseline, and contract;
- signed supported client;
- named support and incident owners;
- restore and rollback tested;
- no open critical issue;
- private/public/Commons data paths verified.

### Add second pilot

- first pilot reached value;
- P0 incidents resolved;
- support under four hours per week;
- onboarding checklist reusable;
- cost receipts working.

### Claim product-market signal

- three paid pilots;
- at least two convert or renew;
- same core workflow reused;
- clear measurable outcome;
- declining implementation burden;
- users return without weekly founder prompting.

### Prepare general availability

- self-serve identity and Organization administration;
- multi-tenant isolation certification;
- billing and entitlements;
- standard legal pack;
- supported release channels;
- published status/support process;
- security review and penetration test;
- repeatable activation and retention.
