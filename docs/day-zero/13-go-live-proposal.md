# Go-live proposal

## Recommendation

Launch Bridge as a controlled, paid, founder-led design-partner pilot. Do not launch as public self-serve or describe the current public-safe web deployment as the full private product.

The repository has a certified Avatar + Commons product loop and a live hosted identity/public boundary. The remaining risks are commercial focus, real-customer operating proof, desktop distribution, external credential certification for DealPilot, and support/legal readiness—not absence of product depth.

## Proposed launch offer

### Bridge Founding Partner Program

- 90-day paid pilot.
- One Organization, up to five users.
- Desktop-first with web review/access.
- Relationship + Task Manager + curated Commons.
- One selected customer workflow.
- Optional DealPilot only after the real Source credential and Google OAuth gate passes.
- Weekly outcome review.
- Approval required for external Actions.
- No unknown Commons publishers.
- Clear Local versus Cloud data map.

Commercial hypothesis:

- $5,000 onboarding and workflow configuration.
- $2,500 per month for up to five users.
- Onboarding fee credited toward an annual agreement if converted.
- Customer pays third-party usage above an agreed included budget.

Pricing should be approved by the founder and tested, not presented as established market truth.

## Launch claim

Use:

> Bridge is Living Software for governed professional work. It connects evidence, people, tasks, and AI capabilities in Modules that adapt around how your team works—while keeping consequential actions visible, attributable, and under your control.

Do not use:

- fully autonomous;
- always watching;
- understands your entire business;
- enterprise-grade compliance;
- private by definition without explaining boundaries;
- marketplace claims before third-party publishing is live.

## First workflow choice

Preferred decision rule:

- If a committed ETA design partner can provide authorized Source access and Google OAuth immediately, launch with the bounded DealPilot thesis → source → deal workflow.
- Otherwise launch with Relationship + Task Manager around one real relationship-follow-up or evidence-to-action workflow. Keep DealPilot out of the contract until its credential gate is proven.

This avoids holding the entire pilot behind a single external integration.

## Go-live plan

### Gate A: founder decisions — two business days

- Approve customer segment and first workflow.
- Select first design partner.
- Approve pilot price and concessions.
- Decide included Modules, model providers, and data locations.
- Name launch owner and incident owner.

### Gate B: release readiness — up to two weeks

- Close any workflow-specific credential gate.
- Produce signed/notarized macOS pilot build or explicitly approve a bounded internal distribution method.
- Verify fresh installation, upgrade, rollback, and removal.
- Re-run exact end-to-end journey with real permitted data.
- Test backup/restore and capability suspension.
- Create standard onboarding, support, and incident runbooks.
- Complete counsel review of pilot agreement, privacy notice, DPA, security exhibit, AI disclosure, and subprocessors.
- Configure per-Organization spend limits.

### Gate C: internal rehearsal — one week

- Operate the workflow daily.
- Introduce one model outage, one Integration denial, one veto, and one rollback.
- Confirm every state is understandable.
- Measure cost and support time.
- Fix launch-blocking defects.

### Gate D: first customer — four weeks

- Onboard one Organization.
- Baseline the workflow before configuration.
- Review outcomes and trust weekly.
- Keep scope frozen except safety, correctness, and directly blocking workflow work.
- Produce a 30-day outcome report.

### Gate E: expand or stop

Add another design partner only if:

- customer completed the target workflow;
- no unresolved P0/P1 security or data issue exists;
- provenance and approval were correct;
- support is below four hours per week;
- variable cost is within budget;
- at least 70% of configuration is reusable.

If those conditions fail, narrow the workflow or correct the platform before adding customers.

## Go/no-go checklist

### Product

- Fresh user reaches first useful outcome.
- Avatar states and approvals are understandable.
- Real data or honest empty states only.
- Correction, veto, pause, and delete paths work.
- Selected workflow has a named success metric.

### Security and privacy

- Auth and tenant boundary proven.
- Private Local Plane path proven.
- Egress and taint fail closed.
- Credentials stored and resolved safely.
- Commons package signature, privacy, and revocation proven.
- No unresolved critical issue.

### Operations

- Signed build and version inventory.
- Deploy, rollback, backup, restore, and offboarding rehearsed.
- Monitoring and spend alerts active.
- Support channel and severity owner active.
- Incident contact list complete.

### Commercial and legal

- Signed pilot charter/order form.
- Pricing and usage budget agreed.
- Privacy, DPA, security, AI, and subprocessors disclosed.
- No unsupported compliance promise.
- Customer baseline recorded.

## Thirty-day deliverables

- One paid design partner live.
- One complete workflow measured against baseline.
- Weekly product, trust, cost, and support scorecard.
- No critical security/privacy incident.
- One repeatable onboarding checklist.
- One generalized capability retained for reuse.
- Written expand, narrow, or stop decision.

## What not to do next

- Do not open public sign-up.
- Do not add a broad Commons publisher program.
- Do not build more Modules to improve the demo.
- Do not make mobile parity a prerequisite for the first desktop pilot.
- Do not accept bespoke external Actions outside the selected workflow.
- Do not spend on growth before retention and support are understood.
