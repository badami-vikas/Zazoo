# Business model and go-to-market

## Commercial thesis

Bridge should sell an outcome-bearing operating layer for high-value knowledge work, not generic AI seats or a marketplace promise.

Customers pay because Bridge:

- reduces repeated coordination and research;
- preserves context across people and time;
- improves the quality and traceability of decisions;
- turns recurring work into governed reusable capability;
- lowers tool fragmentation without demanding an immediate rip-and-replace.

## Initial customer profile

Recommended first segment: founder-led professional teams of 2–15 people doing relationship-heavy, evidence-heavy, recurring work.

Strong signals:

- the owner personally feels the workflow pain;
- each completed outcome is worth hundreds or thousands of dollars;
- evidence and provenance matter;
- work repeats weekly;
- the team uses email, documents, spreadsheets, and several SaaS tools;
- the owner will join weekly feedback;
- the team accepts a desktop-first private pilot.

Avoid initially:

- customers requiring certified compliance before pilot;
- highly distributed enterprise rollouts;
- low-value high-volume consumer workflows;
- teams seeking unsupervised mass outreach;
- customers unwilling to define a measurable target outcome.

## Wedge

Lead with one complete workflow, not “software that builds itself.”

Recommended first commercial wedge:

> Bridge keeps relationship and transaction work connected, turns evidence into governed next actions, and preserves the reasoning behind every decision.

For ETA/search teams, DealPilot can make this concrete. For a broader design partner, Relationship + Task Manager + one custom Module may be the safer first pilot until DealPilot’s live credential gates are completed.

## Packaging

### Founding design-partner pilot

- 90 days.
- One Organization.
- Up to five active users.
- Desktop + web.
- Relationship, Task Manager, Commons-curated capabilities, and one agreed workflow.
- Weekly founder/product session.
- Bounded integration work.
- No SLA beyond the signed pilot charter.
- No autonomous external sending.

Pricing hypothesis: $2,500 per Organization per month, plus a $5,000 onboarding and workflow-design fee that may be credited against an annual conversion. Discounts should buy specific learning rights, case-study permission, prepayment, or reduced support—not be arbitrary.

### Post-pilot team plan

Hypothesis to test after three successful pilots:

- platform fee for Local Plane, governance, core Modules, and support;
- per-active-user fee;
- metered or included AI usage with clear budget controls;
- separately priced premium Modules and assurance;
- private Commons catalog for larger customers.

Do not publish final pricing until actual support, inference, storage, and integration costs are measured.

## Buyer and champion

- Economic buyer: founder, managing partner, team lead, or operations owner.
- Daily champion: operator who owns the selected workflow.
- Security approver: owner or IT/security contact.
- Users: team members who consume recommendations, approve Actions, and correct outcomes.

## Sales motion

Founder-led and diagnostic:

1. Identify one expensive repeated workflow.
2. Quantify current cycle time, error, handoff, and evidence loss.
3. Map tools, permissions, and data sensitivity.
4. Demonstrate the exact governed loop with safe sample or customer-owned data.
5. Agree a 90-day pilot with baseline and success criteria.
6. Configure rather than promise bespoke development.
7. Review outcomes weekly.
8. Convert only if the product proves repeatable value.

## Pilot success story

A credible case study should say:

- what work the team was trying to complete;
- what the baseline cost or delay was;
- which Module and capability changed the work;
- how authority, privacy, and correction were handled;
- measurable improvement;
- which part generalized for the next customer.

Avoid vanity stories about number of prompts, generated pages, or autonomous actions.

## Acquisition channels

Order of effort:

1. Founder network and warm design partners.
2. Practitioner communities in the selected workflow.
3. Expert-led demonstrations using real governed work.
4. Partner referrals from operators, consultants, and implementation specialists.
5. Published case studies and capability playbooks.
6. Commons discovery after the product has active customers.

Paid acquisition is premature until onboarding, retention, and sales conversion are repeatable.

## Competitive positioning

Against chat assistants: Bridge retains governed context and executes through attributable capabilities.

Against automation builders: Bridge begins from work and evidence, then proposes safe Automations.

Against CRMs and project tools: Bridge composes Modules around a shared graph instead of forcing all work into one fixed category.

Against desktop companions: Bridge connects presence to auditable work, Local capture, and deterministic authority.

Against agent platforms: Bridge treats governance, residency, provenance, and correction as the product spine.

## Go-to-market gates

Do not expand segment or channel until:

- at least three design partners complete the same core value loop;
- two retain after the paid pilot;
- median time to first value is under one business day;
- support burden is trending down;
- security and privacy objections have standard answers;
- one Module or capability is reused with minimal custom code;
- variable cost and gross-margin targets are understood.
